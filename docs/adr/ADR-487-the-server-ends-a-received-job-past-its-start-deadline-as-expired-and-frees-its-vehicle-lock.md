# ADR-487: The Server Ends a Received Job Past Its Start Deadline as Expired and Frees Its Vehicle Lock

**Date:** 2026-11-17
**Status:** Accepted (item 7's order gains the store check between the bounds and the key, ADR-501)
**Affects:** design 5.3, 8.8, 12; `api/openapi.yaml` (`POST /v1/jobs`, `JobRequest`, `Job`); `crates/server` (`job_expiry`, `jobs`, `db`, `agent_events`, `job_queue`, `lib`), `crates/server/docs/ngr-server.md`; annotates ADR-476 item 3, ADR-477 items 2 and 6 and ADR-480 item 4

## Context

The delivery queue takes no job whose `start_deadline` has passed (ADR-476 item 3, which left the ending of such a job to later work), but nothing ended it: the row stayed `received` for good and, when it named a VIN, its vehicle lock held the vehicle until the lock's own expiry (ADR-480). `POST /v1/jobs` also refused a `startDeadline` (ADR-477 item 2, since a date could not be parsed without a dependency) and gave every job a deadline 15 minutes after its issue time, so an operator could not ask for a longer or a shorter window. Design 5.3 says a job has a start deadline and that the server judges time by its own clock (16.3). The maintainer decided on 2026-11-17: a periodic task of the server ends jobs past their deadline, the request accepts the deadline within bounds, and a replay with another deadline is a key conflict.

## Decision

1. **One task, every 30 seconds.** `job_expiry::run` is a task of the hub's set (as the delivery queue and the revocation pass are), started by `Server::start` with the default settings and left off by `Server::bind` unless a test turns it on (`with_job_expiry`). The first pass comes one period after the start. The stop ends it in its wait and in its statement (a statement dropped in flight rolls back), and the hub's tasks end before the pool closes, so nothing of it runs against a closed pool. Jobs past their deadline while the server was down are ended by the first pass after the start.
2. **One statement per pass.** In one transaction with the 5-second timeouts (as every pass of the queue), `UPDATE jobs SET state = 'expired', finished_at = now()` for the rows chosen by `WHERE (tenant_id, id) IN (SELECT tenant_id, id FROM jobs WHERE state = 'received' AND finished_at IS NULL AND start_deadline <= now() FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED)`, and in the same statement the deletion of the vehicle lock row that names the ended job, as every end of the queue does (ADR-480 item 6). The tail that deletes the lock is one fragment (`db::delete_own_lock!`), shared by this statement, the retired-key sweep, the too-large end, the rejection and the end an agent reports, instead of a fifth copy (ADR-397). A job with no VIN, a lock row that names another job and a VIN locked in another tenant delete nothing. The failure code, the failure detail, `delivered_at`, `last_reported_at` and the delivery mark are not touched: the end is not a failure.
3. **Only the database's clock, and only `received`.** The comparison is the database's `now()` against the column; the process's clock paces the tick and nothing else. The deadline is reached when `start_deadline <= now()`, the take's complement (it keeps a row while `start_deadline > now()`), so no instant belongs to both. The predicate has no term for `delivered_at`, for the mark or for the lease: a job the server delivered and the agent has not reported past `received` is expired as well. A job in any other state (`precheck` to `cancelRequested`, an end, an agent's recovery end reopened by a report) is never touched, whatever its deadline: the server learns that a job started only from the agent's report.
4. **`SKIP LOCKED`, not a wait.** A row that a take, an answer or an event holds is left for the next pass (a held row costs one period); waiting for it could deadlock with the transaction that holds it and waits for a row this statement locked first. Two servers passing at once end each job once, delete each lock once, and their counts sum to the total.
5. **What the end does not write.** No `agent_events` row and no move of the agent's cursor (the end is the server's, not an event of the agent's, so the agent's numbering is untouched); no `change_feed` row (no end writes one today: design 4.4's feed is for datasets, and a feed of job states for all five ends is separate work); no `audit_log` row (the job-end audit rows are their own work, ADR-484 Consequences). Each pass that ends jobs logs the count at `info`, with no job ID and no VIN.
6. **Failure handling.** A pass that fails (the database unreachable, the 5-second timeout, a statement error) writes nothing, as it is one statement, is logged as one `warn` at most once in 60 seconds, and is tried again at the next tick.
7. **The request's `startDeadline`.** `POST /v1/jobs` accepts `startDeadline` as an RFC 3339 date-time string in the grammar the server already reads a device time in (`agent_events::strict_device_time`, extended to return the seconds): upper-case `T` and `Z`, an offset of at most 15:59, a fraction of one to nine digits that is floored, a second of at most 59, a real calendar date from the year 1. It is normalized to UTC whole seconds. A value that is not a string, is null, or does not parse is 400 with no database access, as every shape error is. The bounds are judged in the transaction against the job's issue time, the floor of the database time of the permission read: the deadline must lie 60 to 86 400 seconds after it, both inclusive, else 422 with the Problem `code` `START_DEADLINE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS` (a year 1 or year 9999 value is well formed and out of bounds). The order is: the key (a replay answers first), the permission (403), the bounds (422), the active instruction key (503); the refusal leaves no row, no audit row, no lock and the key unbound. With no field the deadline is 900 seconds after the issue time as before. The signed instruction and the row carry the same second.
8. **The request hash.** An absent field appends nothing to the hash, so the hash of a request without it is the one made before this decision and an old row still replays. A present field appends the name `startDeadline` and its normalized seconds. A replay with another deadline, a first request with the field and a replay without it, and the reverse (a request that names the default's own second is another request than one that names none) are the existing key conflict, 422 with no code (ADR-477 items 4 and 6; `Outcome::Conflict`). A replay with the same deadline is answered from the stored row with no bounds check, since the key is judged first, even when the deadline has since come closer to the replay's own time than the bounds allow.
9. **The lock follows the deadline.** The lock's expiry stays the job's deadline plus the kind's allowance (ADR-480 item 4), so a lock now lives up to 24 hours plus 10 or 30 minutes from its job's creation, not 25 or 45 minutes. A replayed creation of an expired job answers 202 with `state` `expired` and no `failure` member; the next job for the vehicle is then accepted.
10. **Testing.** The cases are tests against a throwaway PostgreSQL (the statement on every state and combination, the lock tail, the skip, two passes at once, the task's start and stop, a failing pass, the agent's later reports, the request's bounds, forms, hash and replays) and unit tests of the parser, the bounds and the pinned hash of a request without the field.

## Consequences

- **A job the agent started before its deadline, whose first report past `received` has not reached the server, is expired and unlocked while it runs.** The task knows only the state the server holds. An agent that is disconnected, or whose outbox is behind, when the deadline passes has its job ended on the server and its vehicle freed, though the job runs; its later reports are stored and acknowledged and not applied (ADR-481 item 3, the first end is final), so a `writeSettings` row can say `expired` for a write that happened. The next job for the vehicle can take the lock while the first still runs. Reconciling the two views on reconnection is separate work (design 5.3, 15.1), as is the question whether an agent's `expired` that arrives after the server's own is more than a gated warning; this decision leaves `job_state::decide` as it is, so the agent's late `received` is quiet and any other late state is one warning.
- **The retired-key sweep and this task can reach one job together (R4).** A job past its deadline and signed by a key that is no longer active is ended by whichever statement runs first, as `failed` with `instructionKeyRetired` or as `expired`; the lock is deleted once either way. Neither outcome is wrong, and no test asserts which.
- **One statement under the 5-second timeout.** The statement can read the rows through the partial index `jobs_pending` (`received`, `precheck`, `ready`), filters them by deadline and locks the ones it ends, so its cost follows the number of jobs waiting to start, which is small at M3's scale. A backlog large enough to run past the timeout would fail every pass and be retried, with a warning each minute, until the statement is bounded (a limit per pass) or indexed on `(state, start_deadline)`; this decision adds neither a limit nor a migration.
- **No feed and no audit row tell anyone that a job expired.** The operator's `Job` shows it when read or replayed. A feed of job-state changes, for the server's five ends, and the audit rows of the ends are separate work.
- **A shorter lock lifetime is gone.** A request may name a deadline a day away, so the next job for its vehicle is refused with 409 for that long unless the first job ends; nothing lets an operator free the lock (ADR-480 Consequences).
- ADR-476 item 3's sentence that ending an expired job is not the queue's decision stands: the task is not the queue's, and the take still leaves a job at its deadline. ADR-477 item 2's refusal of `startDeadline` and its fixed 15 minutes, and item 6's list of what the hash holds, are replaced by items 7 and 8 above; its other items stand. ADR-480 item 4's "25 and 45 minutes at most" is item 9 above.
